Disease: Tomato Late Blight

Crop: Tomato

Cause:
Tomato late blight is caused by the pathogen Phytophthora infestans.

Symptoms:
- Dark brown to black irregular lesions appear on tomato leaves.
- Lesions may expand rapidly during cool and humid weather.
- White fungal-like growth may appear on the underside of leaves under humid conditions.
- Fruits may develop large brown or dark lesions.
- Severe infection can cause rapid plant decline.

Favorable Conditions:
- High humidity
- Frequent rainfall
- Wet leaves
- Cool to moderate temperatures
- Poor air circulation

Management:
- Remove and destroy severely infected leaves and plants.
- Avoid overhead irrigation.
- Improve air circulation between plants.
- Keep foliage dry when possible.
- Remove infected plant debris.
- Use disease-resistant tomato varieties when available.

Treatment:
Appropriate fungicides may help prevent disease spread when applied according to the product label and local agricultural recommendations. Copper-based fungicides and other registered fungicides may be used depending on local regulations and disease pressure.

Prevention:
- Use healthy planting material.
- Provide adequate spacing.
- Avoid prolonged leaf wetness.
- Monitor plants regularly.
- Remove infected debris from the field.

Important:
Chemical treatment should always follow the product label, local regulations, and agricultural expert recommendations.
